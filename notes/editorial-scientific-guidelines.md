# Editorial and scientific guidelines

These conventions govern the public-facing scientific text of the project and should be applied in subsequent milestones.

1. **Write for the external specialized reader.** Public chapters must be self-contained and must not mention internal milestones, implementation gates, planning discussions, or instructions to developers.
2. **Use formal, affirmative academic prose.** Prefer third-person scientific writing and state the research contribution positively. Avoid formulations centered on what the literature does not do when the contribution can be expressed directly.
3. **Define every acronym at first occurrence.** Examples include UAV, UCAV, RCS, CEM, CFD, FDTD, MoM, FEM, PO, LE-PO, RL-GO, SBR+, MDO, NLP, MINLP, KPLS and UQ.
4. **Identify sources unambiguously.** On first mention, use the full author name or a sufficiently specific bibliographic reference; avoid ambiguous expressions such as “the work of Silva”.
5. **Describe computational burden in terms of resources.** Prefer expressions such as computational-resource demand, processing time, CPU/GPU hours, memory, storage, mesh size and number of solver evaluations. Avoid translating `costly` as “caro/barato” when referring to computation.
6. **Explain fidelity before using multifidelity terminology.** Fidelity is the degree of physical and numerical detail with which a model represents the response of interest. Multifidelity modeling combines models with different levels of detail and different resource demands to exploit broad coverage from lower-demand models and selected reference evaluations from higher-fidelity models.
7. **Keep public text independent of the development plan.** Terms such as M0, M1, current milestone, future milestone, “this chapter will later decide”, or similar planning language must not appear in the eBook narrative.
8. **Treat deterministic mathematical optimization as a research axis within MDO.** Introduce constrained NLP and, when discrete variables are present, MINLP; include gradient-based and deterministic global methods where appropriate. Evolutionary or heuristic methods may be comparative approaches rather than the only optimization paradigm.
9. **Use a precise and affirmative research gap.** The gap should describe the integration pursued by the research: UCAV aero-stealth MDO, mesh-native GNNs, explicit multifidelity learning, angular/polarized RCS, deterministic mathematical optimization, Geometric Algebra and reference-model re-evaluation.
10. **Treat UQ and active learning as optional supporting strategies unless evidence justifies adoption.** Quantification of uncertainty (UQ) must be defined. Active learning must be justified by the need to select additional resource-intensive simulations more efficiently than a fixed sampling plan; it is not a mandatory component by default.
11. **Geometric Algebra is a committed methodological pillar.** It is adopted for geometric and physical representation and for the algebraic formulation of learning and optimization. The project seeks more compact multivector formulations and linear representations when mathematically admissible, while preserving intrinsic physical nonlinearities. AG must not be presented merely as an optional representation.
12. **Do not formulate a hypothesis that decides whether Geometric Algebra is adopted.** The former comparative H4 (GA versus conventional representation) is removed. Ablation may quantify the contribution of AG without making its adoption contingent on that result.
13. **Maintain mathematical rigor when discussing “linearization”.** Maxwell equations in linear media are already linear in the fields; Geometric Algebra unifies and compactifies their representation. Euler equations remain physically nonlinear. Any claimed linearization must refer to a mathematically defined representation, operator, local approximation, or reformulation and must not imply that intrinsic nonlinear physics disappears.
14. **Separate surrogate accuracy from optimization usefulness.** Evaluation must include prediction error, computational-resource use, number of reference-solver calls, end-to-end processing time and quality of re-evaluated Pareto solutions.
15. **Prefer recent, peer-reviewed and technically verifiable references.** Prioritize literature with DOI and measurable evidence, while retaining seminal works when required for mathematical or methodological foundations.
